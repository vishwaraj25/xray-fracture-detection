# FracAtlas: exploratory data analysis

Generated by `src/eda.py`. Figures are in `reports/figures/`.

## Overview

- **4,083 images**, **719 fractured (17.6%)**. The classes are imbalanced, so accuracy alone is misleading: predicting "not fractured" every time scores 82.4%.
- 171 images have more than one annotated fracture.
- 99 images show metal implants, and 396 contain several scans in one image.
- **Data quality:** 2 images are stored in the `Non_fractured` folder but labelled fractured in `dataset.csv`. The CSV label is used. One JPEG is slightly truncated but still readable.
- Most common original sizes: 373×454 (2,704), 454×373 (1,023), 2304×2880 (303). Every image is downscaled once to at most 384px on the longer side.

## Image resolution

| resolution | images | fractured | rate (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| high-res (>1000px) | 351 | 102 | 29.1% (24.6%–34.0%) |
| low-res (≤1000px) | 3,732 | 617 | 16.5% (15.4%–17.8%) |

The images come from a few devices and export settings. High-resolution scans are fractured more often,
so a model could partly learn the scanner instead of the bone. Every image is resized to the same input size before modelling.

## Metal implants

| implant | images | fractured | rate (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| metal implant | 99 | 97 | 98.0% (92.9%–99.4%) |
| no implant | 3,984 | 622 | 15.6% (14.5%–16.8%) |

Metal implants are a strong shortcut: a model can learn "metal means fractured"
without looking at the bone. Results are therefore also reported separately for images without implants.

## Near-duplicates and leakage

FracAtlas has no patient IDs. Perceptual hashing finds **206 groups of near-identical images (510 images)**,
for example the same scan exported twice or the same limb before and after surgery.
Each group is kept inside one split. 29 of these groups contain both labels,
which usually means a follow-up scan of the same patient.
