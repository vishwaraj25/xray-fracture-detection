Model details

What the model does, and how it was tested.

A ResNet-18 neural network estimates how likely it is that an X-ray shows a fracture. Every number below comes from images the model never saw in training. It is a research prototype: it has not been tested in clinical use.

01Data

Two public datasets, both CC BY 4.0. Each was split so that related images never appear on both sides of a split.

DatasetX-raysFracturedSplit
FracAtlas, Bangladesh. Hand, leg, hip, shoulder. 4,083719 (18%)70 / 15 / 15 train, validation, test, grouped by near-duplicate image
GRAZPEDWRI-DX (part 1), Austria. Children's wrists. 5,031about 66%60 / 20 / 20 by patient (1,499 patients)

FracAtlas has no patient IDs, so near-duplicate images were grouped with a perceptual hash (206 groups). This matters: an early version of the project scored 99% on a random split and 7% on new images, because copies of the same X-ray sat in both training and test. Graz images with a cast, metal or an uncertain diagnosis were left out of training.

02Model

  1. 01PrepareGrayscale, padded to a square, 384 × 384 px.
  2. 02ScoreResNet-18, pretrained on ImageNet, fine-tuned on X-rays.
  3. 03AverageThe image and its mirror image are scored and averaged.
  4. 04ExplainA Grad-CAM heat map from the last layer.

Training used augmentation, a loss weighted towards fractures, and early stopping on validation data. A classical baseline (an SVM on HOG and LBP image features) was trained on the same split for comparison.

The demo runs the same network in your browser: it was exported to ONNX with half-precision weights (22 MB). On 24 test X-rays its scores differ from the original by 0.003 on average, and every verdict matches.

06Heat maps

Grad-CAM shows which region drove the score, on a coarse grid of 32-pixel cells. For the 99 test fractures the model flagged, its peak was within one cell of the radiologist's box in 61%, and within 14 pixels in 53%. So it often, but not always, looks at the fracture. The heat map is not an outline of a fracture.

07Limitations

  • Not validated in clinical use, or at any hospital outside its training data.
  • It missed 12 of 111 test fractures. A low score does not exclude a fracture.
  • Many flags are false alarms: 38% of flagged test X-rays had a fracture.
  • The cut-off does not transfer between hospitals. At this cut-off, only 35% of healthy Graz wrists are cleared, because Graz scores run higher.
  • Shortcuts exist: 98% of FracAtlas X-rays with metal implants are fractured, and almost every Graz X-ray with a cast is flagged. A model that never sees the image scores 0.78 ROC-AUC from such labels alone.
  • Only 6 hip and 3 shoulder fractures were in the test set, so those results are uncertain.
  • The model was trained once; another training run would give somewhat different numbers.
